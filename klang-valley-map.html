Peninsular Malaysia Forest Patch Connectivity Project

This interactive map visualises forest patches in the Klang Valley region of Malaysia. Each patch is classified into one of six tiers using a Composite Habitat Suitability Index (CHSI), which combines landscape metrics including total area, core area, contiguity, perimeter-area ratio, and distance to the nearest neighbouring patch. Connectivity values are derived from circuit theory modelling using Omniscape. Click on any patch to view its details.

How to use this map

Getting started

Zoom into any part of the Klang Valley until green forest patches appear on the map. You can also use the search bar in the sidebar to jump to a specific location. The map works best on a desktop browser.

What the patch colours mean

Each patch is coloured according to its conservation tier, which reflects its structural quality as a habitat:

  • Tier 1 (Core habitat) - the most ecologically intact patches, large enough to support resident populations of arboreal animals with substantial buffered interior area
  • Tier 2 (Major stepping stones) - high-quality patches that act as critical hubs or bridges in the movement network
  • Tier 3 (Connected fragments) - moderately connected patches that help link larger areas of forest
  • Tier 4 (Vulnerable edge fragments) - smaller patches with significant edge exposure that are still functionally important
  • Tier 5 (Isolated fragments) - small patches with limited connectivity to the wider network
  • Tier 6 (Isolated micro patches) - highly isolated remnant fragments, generally too small to support resident populations

Use the "Filter by category" checkboxes in the sidebar to show or hide individual tiers on the map.

Clicking on a patch

Click any forest patch to see its details in the sidebar. The panel shows:

  • The conservation tier and a plain-language description of what it means
  • The connectivity rating based on circuit theory modelling (High, Moderate, Low, or Barrier)
  • The distance to the nearest adjacent forest patch

Expand "Show technical details" to see the individual landscape metrics used to calculate the tier. Each metric has a small "i" button you can click to get a plain-language explanation of what it measures and why it matters.

Connectivity rating

The connectivity rating shown for each patch is derived from Omniscape, a circuit theory model that simulates how current flows across the landscape. It reflects how much movement potential passes through or near each patch:

  • High - the patch sits within an active corridor where movement is relatively unimpeded
  • Moderate - some movement potential, but the surrounding landscape presents some resistance
  • Low - limited movement potential due to high landscape resistance
  • Barrier - the patch is surrounded by an effectively impermeable zone such as dense urban development

Potential movement corridors

Click "Show corridors" at the top of the map to reveal lines connecting forest patches to their nearest neighbours. Each line is coloured by the connectivity of the source patch:

  • Yellow - high connectivity
  • Orange - moderate connectivity
  • Red - low connectivity

Use the High, Moderate, and Low buttons that appear alongside "Hide corridors" to toggle each level on or off. A checkmark indicates that level is currently visible. Click any corridor line to see details about that potential crossing in the sidebar.

Filtering patches

Use the "Filter by patch area" tool in the sidebar to show only patches above or below a certain size in hectares. This can help you focus on large core habitats or identify which smaller patches are most at risk.
