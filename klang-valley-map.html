Peninsular Malaysia Forest Patch Connectivity Project

This interactive map shows over 14,000 forest patches extracted from the Klang Valley region in Malaysia. Each patch is classified into one of several tiers using a Composite Habitat Suitability Index (CHSI), which combines key ecological attributes including total area, core area, contiguity, perimeter–area ratio, and Euclidean nearest-neighbour distance. The index standardises these metrics based on the landscape's own statistical distribution and applies weighted scoring to prioritise interior habitat stability and connectivity. Patches are then grouped into six tiers, ranging from large core habitats to highly isolated micro patches. Click on the individual patches to view their details.

How to Use This Map

🗺️ Navigating the map

Zoom in until forest patches appear. Click any patch to see its details in the sidebar. Use the search bar to find specific locations.

🌳 What the patch colours mean

Patches are coloured by conservation tier — light green (Tier 1, highest quality) to dark green (Tier 6, most degraded). Use the Filter by Category checkboxes to show or hide specific tiers.

🔗 Movement pathways

Click Show Movement Pathways to reveal lines connecting each patch to its nearest neighbour. Solid bright lines show connections within gibbon dispersal range (800 m or less). Dashed faint lines are likely too far for a single individual.

🎨 Connectivity colour mode

Click Colour by: Tier to switch patch colours to show connectivity rating (High / Moderate / Low / Barrier) based on circuit theory modelling.

📊 Reading patch details

Clicking a patch shows its tier, connectivity rating, nearest neighbour distance, and pinch point status in plain language. Click Show technical details for full metrics.

🔬 What is a pinch point?

A location where most movement is funnelled through a narrow corridor. Patches with high pinch point coverage are critical — losing them would severely disrupt regional movement.
